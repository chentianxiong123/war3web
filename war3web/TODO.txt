[x] Fractional alpha on geoset visibility  (FIXED)
    Geoset visibility was binary -- render.js did `mesh.visible = a > 0.05`.
    Warcraft III draws the geoset at the track's own alpha, so anything meant to
    fade popped instead. Now the alpha reaches the material, and it is
    multiplied into the authored opacity rather than replacing it, so a geoset
    animation and a SetUnitVertexColor compose (0.5 x 0.5 = 0.25) instead of the
    last writer winning.
    18 of the 1105 converted models carry an alpha strictly between clear and
    solid: the five auras, War Stomp's and Thunder Clap's caster rings, four
    missiles, the three feathers, green smoke, the goblin sapper and the sand
    wave. Trueshot Aura's ring is the plainest case -- authored to run
    0.30 -> 0.535 -> 0.30 and never solid at any point, so it had been drawn at
    full opacity for its entire life.
    A model that never fades still pays nothing: materials are only cloned when
    a geoset is actually part-way through one.
    Found while fixing it: two of the eighteen -- Devotion Aura and the Voodoo
    Aura -- have no glTF animation at all, because nothing on them moves and the
    geoset alpha *is* the animation. No clip meant no mixer, no mixer meant no
    time to sample, so they were frozen on their first frame, which for both is
    fully transparent. They now get a clock of their own from the sequence's
    duration. tools/geoset_test.mjs, 15 checks.

[x] Corpses vanish instead of dissolving  (FIXED)
    Reported from play. Warcraft III fades a body out over Decay Flesh
    and Decay Bone through the *material's* alpha track (KMTA) -- a different
    track from the geoset alpha above. mdx.py parsed it and mdx2gltf.py threw it
    away, so the client had nothing to fade with and a corpse held full opacity
    until its timer ran out.
    Now exported per sequence off the layer pick_layer chose, so it is the alpha
    of the surface actually drawn rather than of a team-colour underlay, and
    multiplied into the same composition as the geoset track and any tint.
    Worth keeping the scale honest: of 90 model/sequence pairs whose material
    fades to clear, only 25 do it while the geoset wearing it is still drawn --
    those are the corpses this visibly dissolves. In the other 65 the geoset
    alpha has already culled the mesh by then, and there this changes nothing on
    screen. 133 of 250 sampled unit models animate the track at all.
    Found while fixing it: Trueshot Aura animates *both* tracks, and geoset_test
    was asserting a raw opacity that only accounted for one. Its ring is
    authored at 0.534 geoset x 0.8 material = 0.427, which is what the game
    draws; the test now asserts the geoset factor it is actually about, plus the
    product. tools/dissolve_test.mjs, 9 checks.

[~] The ground meets the cliffs badly  (HALF FIXED)
    Reported from play: at the seam between a cliff's floor and the
    baked ground the two are visibly different resolutions and read as unblended.
    Two causes found, both fixed:
      - bake_ground.py baked at PX=32 while Blizzard's own tile variations are
        64x64, so every ground tile was resampled to half size before it was
        ever drawn. Now PX=64, the source art's own resolution: _ground.png goes
        3072x2048 -> 6144x4096 (2.1 MB -> 8.3 MB), 0.25 -> 0.50 texels per world
        unit. Going higher would only upscale -- more bytes, no more detail.
      - the cliff textures ask for max anisotropy and the ground never did. At
        an RTS camera angle the ground is seen edge-on, so it smeared while the
        cliff beside it stayed sharp. It asks for the same now.
    FIXED 2026-09-07: the boundary also ignored CliffTypes.groundTile. The
    bake now uses the cliff's matching Ysqd tile at 697 boundary vertices and
    blends into the surrounding painted terrain through the original masks.
    Ramp interiors keep their painted ground. The source map is not modified.
    STILL OPEN: the cliff surface carries 256 texels over a single 128-unit
    cell -- 2.0 per unit against the ground's 0.50 -- so a 4x mismatch remains,
    and matching it would mean baking at 4x the pixels from 64px source art,
    which is upscaling, not detail. Whether that is worth it is a judgement
    call and wants a look at the real thing first.

[x] Two emitter flags nothing read, and one that is not established  (FIXED)
    Asked the same way the unread ability fields were asked: which of the
    fields tools/mdx2gltf.py exports does the renderer never read? Across the
    1105 converted models, four particle flags and one light field came back.

    Unfogged -- and the fog itself      2493 emitters carry the flag, 152 set it,
        and not one particle was fogged at all, because the emitter shaders are
        raw ShaderMaterials with no fog in them. That cost nothing while the
        world's fog was a soft blue starting at 4200. It started costing the day
        the fog became the map's own black starting at 3000: distant effects sat
        bright against a black horizon. Now the 2341 that do not set the flag
        fade into it and the 152 that do are left alone.
        Two things worth writing down. An additive emitter cannot mix toward the
        fog colour the way an opaque surface does -- adding grey to a spark
        brightens it -- so it fades toward nothing instead, which is the same
        thing whenever the fog is black. And three.js only refreshes fogColor,
        fogNear and fogFar for the material types it ships, so `fog: true` alone
        leaves a ShaderMaterial's uniforms wherever they were built; they are
        copied from the scene each frame instead, which also means an emitter
        follows a SetTerrainFogEx that arrives after it was created.
        Only linear fog. The exponential styles carry a density rather than two
        planes and this map asks for style 0.

    SortPrimsFarZ                       220 emitters set it, and it only changes
        the picture where blending is order-dependent: addition is commutative,
        so the 186 additive ones look identical sorted or not and are left
        alone, paying nothing. That leaves 32 blended point emitters, which now
        get an index buffer sorted far to near each frame. Only the index is
        reordered, never the attributes, so the slot pool spawn() and update()
        share is untouched. The two blended TAIL emitters are left as they were:
        their geometry is already indexed as quads and reordering it is a
        different job for two models.

    LineEmitter -- NOT established, 385 emitters in 128 models
        The obvious reading is that the emitter is a line rather than a plane,
        and the models say otherwise: of the 385 that set the flag, only 39 have
        a zero width and 66 a zero length, against 558 and 555 of the 2108 that
        do not set it. The footprint is if anything LESS degenerate on the line
        emitters, and most carry equal non-zero width and length. So the flag is
        about the emission direction rather than the shape, its meaning lives in
        the game engine rather than in any file here, and it is left unread
        rather than guessed at.

    XYQuad                              carried by every emitter and set by NONE
        of the 2493, so there is nothing to implement.

    attStart on an omni light -- MEASURED AND NOT A DEFECT
        114 of the 116 lights carry an attenuation start and nothing reads it,
        which is true and turns out not to matter. Warcraft III holds a light at
        full brightness to attStart and then falls linearly to zero at attEnd;
        three.js gives a point light two knobs, distance and decay, and the
        curve itself is fixed in the shader every lit material uses. With decay
        at 0 that curve is (1 - (d/D)^4)^2, which is flat near the centre and
        rolls off toward D -- the same shape, by luck rather than design.
        Fitted against Warcraft III's curve for the 80/200 shape that 114 of the
        116 lights carry:
              d=80   (attStart)   wc3 1.000   ours 0.949
              d=120               wc3 0.667   ours 0.758
              d=160               wc3 0.333   ours 0.349
              d=190               wc3 0.083   ours 0.034
        Worst disagreement anywhere is 0.093 of full brightness, rms 0.046.
        Refitting the one knob available moves D from 200 to 197 and improves
        the worst case to 0.077 -- a 1.5% change in radius, not worth making.
        An exact match needs our own light model, which means our own shader for
        every lit material. Recorded so this is not reopened.
        The thing in this area actually worth measuring is the POOL: it holds
        eight slots and an effect that finds it full goes unlit. On a map where
        a dozen effects appear a second that is a likelier cause of a missing
        light than the falloff curve. Not measured yet.

    tools/render_test.mjs, 26 checks, four of them new here -- read off the real
    materials and the real index buffer in a browser, because a uniform that is
    computed and never bound is exactly what a data-only check would pass.

    cosine (full depth at the centre, nothing at the rim, zero slope at both

    files. Nothing else in the crater is a guess.

    hole looks for ten seconds; it cannot accumulate and dent the map.

[x] THE CAST TIMELINE  (FIXED 2026-09-08)
    castAbility resolved a spell on the frame it was ordered and fired all five
    spell events back to back in one call. Warcraft III does neither: a spell
    is an ORDER. The caster walks into range, turns, starts its spell
    animation, and the effect lands at the unit's cast point; a channelled
    spell then holds the caster until it is done or broken off. The five
    events mark those moments, and the map's own triggers sit on them.

    Measured off the script, so this is what the map expects rather than what
    a manual says. TriggerRegisterAnyUnitEventBJ appears once per event; the
    per-spell triggers hang off those four:

      CHANNEL  5: A01G, A00W, A00L, A03K, A060. Four of them CREATE the ritual
               (A00L raises 24 h009 around the caster, A060 raises 30 dummies,
               A03K two pairs of e006/e007, A01G a peasant and pauses the
               target) and then WAIT -- A01G's action is BO(3.) against its
               own acas of 3.0
      ENDCAST  6: A01G, A00L, A03K, A060, A04C, A04X. Five of them REMOVE what
               CHANNEL created (ForGroupBJ over KO(type) -> RemoveUnit); the
               sixth, A04X, sets udg_integer11 = 1 to stop a spin loop
      FINISH   1: A022 pauses, hides and invulnerables the caster

    the right field takes the heroes with no impact sound from 16 of 26 to 3,

[x] Off-screen units were skinned, uploaded and drawn every frame  (FIXED)
    The suspect was skinning; the measurement says it was skinning done many
    times over for units nobody could see. Three things stacked, all measured
    in tools/fps_test.mjs (new rows: skeleton updates, scene matrices, views
    off screen; MOBS=300 fills the field through a new debugSpawn message):
      - Every geoset is loaded with frustumCulled off, and in three.js the
        skeleton update and its bone-texture upload sit *inside* that test
        (WebGLRenderer.projectObject -> objects.update -> skeleton.update). So
        every skinned unit in the match was re-skinned, re-uploaded and drawn
        whether or not it was near the screen. The frustum test render() was
        already doing per view only gated the mixers.
      - SkeletonUtils.clone makes a Skeleton per SkinnedMesh even when the
        source shared one (every converted glb has exactly one skin). A unit
        of four skinned geosets carried four skeletons over the same bones and
        paid four bone-matrix passes and four texture uploads a frame: 1412
        skeleton updates a frame for 397 units.
      - The 387 scenery doodads are skinned models too (city walls, plants),
        never animate, and were re-skinned, re-uploaded and had every bone
        recomposed every frame regardless.
    Now: a unit's model object is hidden while its bounding sphere is outside
    the frustum, with the radius taken from the MDX's own extents (model plus
    every sequence, the same bounds Warcraft III culls with; median 370 model
    units across the 588 unit models, against the 220 the old test guessed),
    and its matrix walk is skipped while hidden. cloneModel folds the clones'
    skeletons back onto one per model. Static doodads are frustum-culled by
    the same bounds, have their skeleton computed once, and their holder's
    matrix walk runs once. The geosets keep frustumCulled off on purpose:
    their bind-pose bounds say nothing about where an animation reaches.
    Measured, headless SwiftShader, 397 units on the same 300-mob layout:
                                 before      after
      draw calls                   2045       1232
      skeleton updates / frame     1412        281
      skeleton CPU                 5.3 ms     2.0 ms
      scene matrix walk            9.1 ms     6.5 ms
      renderer.render CPU         32.0 ms    18.5 ms
      textures held               1613        668
    The GPU side of the bone uploads is invisible under SwiftShader and is the
    part a real card would have felt most; worth a look at the ` readout on
    the 4080 that started this. The 273 units that layout leaves in frame are
    what the remaining matrix walk and the 5 ms of mixers are; a real match
    has far fewer on screen at once.

[x] Warcraft III's own hero-death warning  (FIXED)
    "Our hero has fallen!" is the engine's, not the map's: nothing in
    war3map.j mentions it, which is why it had never been ported. UISounds.slk
    carries HeroDies<Race> for your own hero and AllyHeroDies<Race> for an
    ally's, all 11 rows already converted to ogg by the existing audio step.
    Three details that make it more than "play a sound on hero death": the
    voice is the *listening* player's race rather than the dead hero's, so the
    row is chosen per client and the server only reports the death; there is no
    enemy row in the table at all, so Warcraft III is silent when the other side
    loses one, and so are we; and it is not a 3D sound -- it plays at its own
    volume wherever the hero fell, because the voice is yours and not the
    battlefield's. 25 of the 26 heroes are race human and one is orc, so the
    race is read from unittypes rather than assumed.
    The rows also carry Flags, and every one of them is NODUPLICATES: Warcraft
    III will not start the warning while it is already playing, which in a map
    where heroes die constantly is the difference between a warning and a
    pile-up. Honoured. CHANNELFULLPREEMPT is deliberately not -- it decides what
    to evict when a mixer channel is full, and there is no channel model here.
    The premise was checked rather than assumed: mapmakers ask how to *remove*
    this sound, so it fires in custom maps by default, and FOC never suppresses
    it -- zero VolumeGroupSetVolume, zero SetSoundVolumeBJ, no VOLUME_GROUP_*
    anywhere in war3map.j. What is still unverified is the exact firing rule in
    a 10-player free-for-all; worth a listen in the real client.
    tools/warnsound_test.mjs, 18 checks, covering all four branches.
    Shipped broken first time and caught in play: the client passed
    `/assets/${file}` to playUI, but audio.load prefixes /assets/ itself, so the
    URL doubled, 404'd, and the game stayed silent. Every check still passed --
    they asserted that playUI was *called*. The test now drives a real hero
    death through a debug kill and asks the mixer whether the clip decoded,
    which is the only assertion that could have failed.

[x] No licence on the repo.  (FIXED)
    MIT, and the reasoning is worth keeping because copyleft was the tempting
    alternative and the argument against it here is specific rather than
    general. GPLv3's usual weakness for a hosted game -- that running a service
    is not distributing -- barely applies, since the client is shipped to every
    player's browser and that IS distribution; AGPLv3 would close even that gap.
    What settles it is that neither buys protection against a risk that exists:
    nobody can run this commercially whatever the licence says, because running
    it at all needs Blizzard's archives and the map author's file. So copyleft
    would add friction for the readers this repository is actually for, and
    would guard a door that copyright on other people's material already holds
    shut. Nothing in the dependency tree constrained the choice either way --
    three and ws are MIT, puppeteer-core is Apache 2.0.
    LICENSE carries a scope section naming what the terms do NOT cover, the
    README links it beside the "source only" section that already said the same
    thing about the assets, and package.json declares it so tooling stops
    reading the project as unlicensed.
